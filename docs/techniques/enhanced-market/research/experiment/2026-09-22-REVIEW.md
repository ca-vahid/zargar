## Market context

A narrow, directionless digestion day, as the author's morning note predicted ("flat open after a huge breakout...
most likely digestion"). SPY −0.08% on a 0.33% range, IWM −0.17%, QQQ +0.88%. Levels were tested and failed in both
directions, which is the least favourable tape for a level-bounce and level-reject method.

## Where the difference between the books came from

**On the two trades both books took, the bundle changed nothing.** INTC and OKLO entered at the same price, stopped at
the same price, and lost the same amount in both books (−144.38 together). The admission gate, the preparation policy
and P-06 made no difference to either.

**The whole gap is the experiment's extra trades.** Four fills came from symbols only the rules-only preparation
admitted - SOXS, CRWV, WFC and ADBE - and all four stopped out, for −124.70. The baseline's one trade the experiment did
not take, ASTS, lost −26.00. So the experiment lost more because it took more trades, and on this day more trades meant
more stops.

That is one observation about the bundle's selection, not a finding. Rules-only preparation armed 119 plans against the
model-reviewed 42; whether the extra admissions are worse on average needs many sessions, and a digestion day is the
worst sample to judge breakouts on.

## The nine trades: stop too tight, or wrong direction?

For each setup I asked what price did **after** the stop. If it went on to the first target, the stop was the problem.
If it kept going against the position, the stop did its job and the entry was wrong.

**Five of seven setups were wrong on direction, and their stops saved money** - price kept running 2.6R to 8.9R
against them afterwards. Widening those stops would have made those losses larger, not rescued them.

**Two of seven were stopped by noise and then worked.** CRWV and OKLO both reached their first target after stopping
out. They are also the two where the stop was smallest relative to the stock's own movement - CRWV's stop was
narrower than a single minute's average range.

## A pattern worth registering, not acting on

**Six of the nine stops are exactly 0.50% of entry.** That is not coincidence. The stop buffer is
`max(2 x tolerance, 0.5% of the anchor, 0.25 x ATR)`, and when an entry sits at the level with no deeper invalidating
structure found, the stop becomes the level plus that buffer. The 0.25 x ATR term almost never exceeds 0.5%, so the
floor decides - regardless of how volatile the stock is.

This is the same shape as the 2026-08-23 finding in TRADING-RULES ("a fixed-percent stop in costume"). That fix made
stops anchor below the invalidating structure, which works when structure is found. Today shows the case it does not
cover: **when no invalidation is found, the stop falls back to a flat 0.5%, and that is volatility-blind.**

The two stops that were too tight were the two where 0.5% was smallest against the stock's volatility. The four
other flat-0.5% stops were on quieter names, where 0.5% was 2.3 to 2.9 times the minute's range, and there the entry,
not the stop, was the problem.

**Hypothesis, for the register rather than the code:** a stop floor scaled to the name's volatility would widen stops on
high-volatility names and leave quieter names alone, with position size shrinking to hold dollar risk constant. It would
have changed two trades today, in opposite directions of certainty: it may have kept CRWV and OKLO alive to their
targets. Two cases from one digestion day are nowhere near enough, and this is exactly the kind of change that fits one
day's losers and fails the next. It needs a preregistered test over many sessions before anyone touches `stop_buffer`.

## Defects found (operational, not strategy)

1. **BRK.B is armed but blind.** It produced 42 of the day's 113 stale-bars errors, yet 600 one-minute BRK.B bars were
   stored for the session. The bars exist; the armed plan never received them. The dot in the ticker is the obvious
   suspect, since feeds disagree between `BRK.B`, `BRK-B` and `BRK/B`, but that is a lead, not a finding. Two BRK.B plans
   were armed that could never have traded. Worth a proper investigation off-hours.
2. **Correction: CRWV's stop does carry a reference.** An earlier version of this review said it did not. That came from
   my own lookup script matching the wrong trigger id and printing nothing; the plan records `above_resistance`. The real
   observation is more useful: CRWV rejected off a resistance level with **one touch**, carried over from a prior plan, as
   a single-point zone. A one-touch level is the weakest kind the method recognises, and it is the trade whose stop was
   narrower than a minute's range. Worth watching whether one-touch carried levels lose disproportionately.
3. **The capture recorder still reports `quote_time_skew` on 15 observations** after the clock fix. The Team2 desk
   independently found that OPRA `source_ts` is the poll time rather than the vendor time; that is the likely source.
   It does not affect trading, but it limits how much of the executable-profit measurement can be scored.

## What went right

- **The clock fix is confirmed by behaviour**, not argument: the experimental book placed 12 orders and filled 6.
- **The measurement path works for the first time on a real position.** The experimental book's capture status is
  `ok` with 80% coverage, and it shows the executable peak was only +2.92 against a −269 finish: those positions never
  meaningfully moved into profit. That is the executable-profit recorder doing exactly what it was built to show.
- **Every check fired on schedule without anyone watching**: the clock check, the two-book verification, the
  post-replan check, three exception checks and the close report.
- **Both books were flat at the close** with no stuck exit and no unmanaged position.
